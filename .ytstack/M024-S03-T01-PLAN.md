## What this does

A step that only the human may perform (a production action, a check in a GUI, anything behind their credentials) becomes one script in stages. The human starts it with one short command. The agent writes it, verifies it without running it and never executes it, a part of it or a command taken from it.

`template.sh` carries a header (Handover, Purpose, Prepared by, Prepared at, Changes, Undo) and a helper block with five calls: `stage`, `check` (read-only, a failure stops the script), `confirm` (only the word yes typed at a terminal goes on), `mutate` (runs only after a confirm in its stage) and `manual` (waits for done or skip). At every exit the script writes a receipt next to itself: what ran with its exit code, and what did not run. The receipt records calls as written in the file, never values or output.

`bin/ytstack-handover new <path> --title <title>` copies the template. `check <script>` is the static verification: `bash -n` passes, the helper block is the template's byte for byte, every line outside it is one helper call, a plain assignment or a comment, every `mutate` has a `confirm` earlier in its stage, there is at least one stage, and the header fields are filled. It also refuses unannounced `sudo`, a pipe into a shell, `rm -rf`, `eval` and a token-looking string in an assignment.

The skill establishes the step from the plan, asks once where handover scripts live (`handover_dir:` in `PREFERENCES.md`), hands over one command line and reads the receipt when it comes back. A receipt never closes a gate.
