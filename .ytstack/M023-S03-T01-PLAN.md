# M023-S03-T01 -- Task Plan

**Summary:** `code-review`: round 1 complete, round 2 on the fix, round 3 a diagnosis; the reviewer states the assumed load, gives a concrete case and "if skipped" per finding, respects a `shortcut:` comment and the project's severity policy, closes with "Not checked"; the round is recorded in the task plan.

## Files

- Modify: `skills/code-review/SKILL.md` (preamble: `REVIEW_ROUND` and `LAST_REVIEWED_HEAD`; the section "Bounded review (ytstack)")

## What this does

The preamble counts the `- Round <n>:` lines under `## Review` in the task plan. It prints `REVIEW_ROUND` (the count plus one) and `LAST_REVIEWED_HEAD` (the head of the last recorded range, `none` when it names no commit).

Round 1 reviews the whole change and is meant to be complete. Round 2 reviews only the commits since `LAST_REVIEWED_HEAD` and gets the findings of round 1. A new finding counts there only when the fix introduced it, or when it is a serious defect with a concrete trigger: a permission that no longer holds, data that can be lost, a wrong result. That one still blocks. From round 3 on no reviewer is dispatched. The skill diagnoses why the review does not converge (scope, conflicting rules or solution) and puts that to the human in one question.

The reviewer opens with what the change does and with `Assumed load:`, taken from the project's documents or marked as assumed. Every finding has the file and lines, a concrete case, the smallest fix and `If skipped:`. A scale finding is rated against the assumed load. A `shortcut:` comment that names its limit is a decision. The review closes with `Not checked:`. Where the project has a severity policy, that policy rates the findings: a non-blocking finding goes to Follow-ups, and a risk a person has to accept becomes a gate. After the findings are handled the skill appends one line per round to the task plan.
