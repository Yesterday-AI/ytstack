## What this does

`ytstack-upkeep [-C <dir>] [--question] [--open-days <n>] [--asked-days <n>] [--lane-hours <n>]` reads a project's `.ytstack/` and prints one report with seven sections, each with a count and its items:

- spec: the failures of `bin/ytstack-spec lint` and the areas `bin/ytstack-spec drift` lists, when the project has a technical spec
- state: the findings of `yt_state_lint`
- gates: the open gate lines of the plans (`- [ ] G## -- ...`, the pattern of `reassess-roadmap`); a gate whose `lapses:` holds a date in the past is marked as lapsed
- questions: the entries of `bin/ytstack-questions list` that are open for more than `--open-days` days (default 3) or were asked more than `--asked-days` days ago (default 7)
- lanes: the rows of `LANES.md` in a held status whose claim is older than `--lane-hours` hours (default 24)
- runtime: the problems of `bin/ytstack-runtime check`
- review policy: the problems of `bin/ytstack-risk check`, only when that script exists beside this one

It exits 0 with `nothing needs a look` when every count is 0, and 10 when one is not. It only reads.

With `--question`, and only when something needs a look, it writes one question file through `bin/ytstack-questions new` ("Upkeep found N things that need a look") and puts the report under its `## Context`. It writes none while an earlier upkeep question is open or asked; that one is recognised by the start of its title.

The suite builds fixture projects in a temporary directory: a clean one, one case per section, the thresholds, `--question` with its dedupe. The two branches of the review policy section run on a copy of the scripts in a stand-in directory, once without and once with a stand-in `ytstack-risk`.
