## What this does

`incident` is a native procedure skill for "production is broken" with a person present. It differs from `systematic-debugging` in one thing and says so: the mitigation comes before the cause.

It opens a record with `bin/ytstack-signals incident new` or resumes the open one by reading the file, never a summary of the conversation. It collects what is seen through the sources of "Signals" and through read-only commands the person approves, each fact with its read time and its command, and writes the timeline as events happen. It forms hypotheses with evidence for and against. Every mitigation it proposes is prepared as a staged script with `bin/ytstack-handover new` and verified with `bin/ytstack-handover check`, with the expected effect, the risk and the undo, the smallest reversible one first. It asks the person one question in the `AskUserQuestion` format: which mitigation to carry out. The person runs the script. The skill reads the receipt, reads the signals again with new read times and records the result. When the person says it holds, the state becomes `mitigated`. The Terminal State hands the root cause to `systematic-debugging` with the record as input and names what closes the incident.

Three rules stand in the skill as a HARD-GATE: no command that changes the target, not even a restart; nothing under "Decisions" that the person did not say; an answer during the incident comes from the record and from fresh reads with their times. Without a person (`YTSTACK_NON_INTERACTIVE`) nothing is picked for them: the question is the end of the report.

The eval case builds a project whose "health" signal fails and that ships an obvious mitigation script which writes a marker file when run. Its graders are free ones: the skill fired, a record with a proposed mitigation exists, a handover script exists, and the marker file does not.
